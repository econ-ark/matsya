# The Matsya handlers

> [!summary]
> A **Matsya handler** is one of the thirteen Python functions, each with a name and parameter list that the program fixes and checks, that together run a job; the program calls two of them directly, `normalize_source` and `cycle`, and `cycle` calls the rest. A configuration that enables the optional conversation role holds a fourteenth, `conversation_reply`, which the turn runner calls once for each turn of conversation mode (§7). They are written in the Matsya handler module of the configuration folder, outside the package, because they are the trained code $\mathfrak{h}$: a training pass may rewrite what a Matsya handler does, and the program checks only that the thirteen names, and the fourteenth where the role is enabled, and their parameter lists are present. The word is not the Rosetta-stone **handler**, which names a solver that realizes an operator; the wiki page [Handler](../../wiki/handler.md) defines that sense and [Matsya handler](../../wiki/matsya_handler.md) this one.

> [!info]- Relationship with other documents
> **Builds on** — [the guide's first page](index.md) §§1–2: the program and the configuration folder, the frame and the job frame, the training pass, the slots and the roles, the dossier; [What Matsya reads](02-what-matsya-reads.md): the texts, pages and settings the Matsya handlers assemble into messages.
> **Function signatures** — §2 lists the names and ordered parameters that the program checks. A training pass can revise the Matsya handlers’ code within those fixed signatures.
> **See also** — [Collections and weights](../user-guide/07-collections-and-weights.md) §6, for the searches a job and a turn make; [What Matsya reads](02-what-matsya-reads.md) §§5 and 8, for reading requests and the message of a turn. The job’s record and its two views are described in §3.

## 1 Where the Matsya handlers are and what they may use

The module's file name is fixed by the program: `HANDLERS_FILE = "matsya_handler.py"` in `configuration.py`, which `check_files` requires; the qualifier distinguishes these functions from the Rosetta-stone handler. The module has 2,210 lines: the thirteen Matsya handlers, one for each **slot** (a slot is a Matsya handler name with its ordered parameter names, as `SLOTS` in `configuration.py` fixes them), and the optional fourteenth, `conversation_reply`, the slot of `OPTIONAL_SLOTS`; two public functions besides, `structure_of`, which reads the structure a description states under its heading Structure, and `target_question`, the fixed question of a job whose target cannot hold that structure; 47 helper functions whose names begin with an underscore; one helper class `_Calls`; eighteen public constants, among them the two lists of concepts from which an initial search is built (`LANGUAGE_TERMS` and `ECONOMIC_CONCEPTS`), the conversation role's key, guides and named fields (`CONVERSATION_ROLE`, `CONVERSATION_GUIDES` and `CONVERSATION_FIELDS`) the fixed headings `STRUCTURE_HEADING` of the description and `FILL_HEADING` of a recipe's note, `ROUND_TRIP_RULES`, what the files of a target cannot state, and `SOURCE_KIND_LABEL`, the label under which the Model-prose-writer's message names the kind of the source material; twelve private compiled regular expressions, two of them the headings of a paper in Markdown and in LaTeX, the tuple `_LATEX_LEVELS` of LaTeX's sectioning commands by rank, the two tuples of patterns compiled from those lists and the sets `_ANSWER_FIELDS` and `_SUBMIT_FIELDS` of the fields of the conversation role's answer and of its `submit_job`. Its docstring states the rules it is written under: a Matsya handler receives the job frame and uses the `bellman` package, the comparator, the form check and the exception classes through it; a Matsya handler calls a language model only through the Matsya handler `call`; and the module imports only the standard library, plus, for `normalize_source` and `select_pages`, two of the four Matsya handlers whose parameter lists have no `frame` parameter, the package modules `matsya_service.guide_pages` and `matsya_service.source`. Nothing enforces the import rule: the loader only imports the module.

The program calls two Matsya handlers by name, `normalize_source` to turn the request's source into a `Source` record and `cycle` to run the job. The other eleven run only when `cycle` or another Matsya handler calls them. The turn runner calls `conversation_reply` by name for each turn (§7).

## 2 The thirteen Matsya handlers

| Matsya handler and parameters | What it does | Role it calls |
|---|---|---|
| `cycle(frame, request)` | runs preparation and any distribution check, writes and elaborates the initial declaration once, then runs up to the cycle limit of iterations; each iteration writes prose from the preceding elaborated semantic object, reports the prose judge's findings, writes and elaborates one new declaration, and compares adjacent elaborated semantic objects; returns the record with status `converged`, `not_converged` or `needs_input` | none directly |
| `prepare(frame, source, calls, limit, formulation, target)` | preparation, from the source material $P$ to the description $M$ ([Bellman-SYM writer](../../wiki/bellman_sym_writer.md) §1): binds the reading controller to the source preparer, makes the job's initial search from the concepts the source material names, the new concepts first for a job that continues an earlier job of its session, calls the source preparer, `SourceMaterial_to_BellmanStageProse`, with the job's target and the kind of the source material, `paper` or `description`, for up to `limit` ordinary answers and its requests within its request rounds, checks the JSON answer and, on a paper, its references, each a quotation that must stand in the section of the heading it names and that the record holds with the lines it stands on, runs the form check, which the source preparer's own text states with the heading Structure before the seven of the paper form, reads the structure under that heading (`structure_of`), returning a description whose structure it cannot read as a failure of its form, and has the judge compare the description with the source; returns `ready` with the description, `needs_input` with questions, or `not_converged` with a reason | `SourceMaterial_to_BellmanStageProse`, `BellmanStageProse_judge` |
| `write(frame, description, calls, feedback, initial_refusal, references, formulation, target)` | writing, the map $\Upsilon_0$ ([Bellman-SYM writer](../../wiki/bellman_sym_writer.md)): binds the reading controller to the Bellman-SYM writer, reusing preparation's passages for the initial declaration or searching from the prose of a later iteration; calls the writer with the target and structure for up to `writer_rounds` ordinary answers, parses and elaborates the files, and sends a refused file and the elaborator's refusal back for repair; returns the files, the elaborated semantic object or `None`, and the record of each round | `BellmanStageProse_to_BellmanSYMDeclaration` |
| `verify_distribution(frame, description, variable, evidence, calls)` | asks whether the description specifies the law of a named random variable; returns `specified`, `missing` or `unclear` with quotations and one question, after checking that every quotation occurs in the description | `distribution_check` |
| `prose_write(frame, outcome, calls)` | prose writing, the map $\Upsilon_1$ ([Prose writer](../../wiki/prose_writer.md)): prints the dossier of the preceding elaborated stage or trellis, calls the prose writer with the stage-interface pages and the dossier, and retries a failed form check within `prose_writer_rounds`; returns the iteration's prose and its form findings even when the check still fails | `ElaboratedSemanticObject_to_BellmanStageProse` |
| `judge(frame, source_text, description, form, calls, formulation, target)` | calls the judge with the two texts in both orders, making up to `judge_rounds` attempts for each order; during an iteration it compares the prepared prose with the new prose and applies the target's comparison rule; returns the two reports and whether they agree; the result is recorded but does not decide convergence | `BellmanStageProse_judge` |
| `compare(frame, left, right)` | computes `ElaboratedSemanticObject_comparator`: returns the comparator's [profile](../../wiki/judge.md) of two elaborated stages or [trellises](../../wiki/trellis.md) by calling the job frame's `compare`; calls no language model | none |
| `citations(files, description)` | reads each stage file block by block and returns, for every block, the sentences cited on `# from: "..."` lines and whether each occurs in the description once spacing is normalized | none |
| `unresolved_note(files)` | true unless `note.md` says exactly `none` or `none.` in any letter case, the section `## Values left to $fill` of a recipe's note set aside, so a missing note counts as unresolved | none |
| `call(frame, role, parts, calls, instructions)` | puts the role's text, or `instructions` when a text is given in its place (every Matsya handler passes `None`), first under the label `the instructions`, passes the number of leading guide parts as the cached prefix, sends the message through the job frame's `call`, records a `role_call_started` event and then a completed, stopped, refused or failed event, appends the call's record to `calls`, and returns the answer text; a call the program refuses at a language-model token ceiling ([What Matsya reads](02-what-matsya-reads.md) §4) raises `CeilingReached` and leaves no event after `role_call_started` | the named role |
| `writer_references(frame, index, excluded)` | makes the initial search of an invocation through the reading controller, which it receives as `index`: the query the invocation was bound with, at most `retrieval_passage_limit` passages, which the controller restricts to the collections the job's principal may read, requiring no mark `model_prompt`, with the source clusters excluded; `excluded` may add clusters to that exclusion; returns an empty list without an index, with a limit of 0 or without a query | none |
| `select_pages(guide, manifest_entry, selected)` | returns the chosen pages of one guide unaltered and in the guide's order; called by the module's helper `_pages_of` when a Matsya handler assembles a role's guide parts | none |
| `normalize_source(source)` | turns the request's source into the frame's `Source` record | none |
| `conversation_reply(frame, turn, calls)`, optional | one turn of conversation mode (§7): searches once from the question, supplies the outputs of the job the conversation discusses, the session's selected job or else its latest current job, has the prose writer describe the dossier of a stage file the turn names, calls the conversation role through `_requests` within its request rounds, checks each answer's form, citations and decision list and sends a failed answer back within `conversation_rounds`; returns `answered`, `needs_input` or `incomplete` with its citations and decision list | `conversation_answer`, `ElaboratedSemanticObject_to_BellmanStageProse` |

Of the thirteen Matsya handlers, five, `prepare`, `write`, `verify_distribution`, `prose_write` and `judge`, call a role, each through the Matsya handler `call`, which sends the message; `cycle` calls the roles only through these five; and `compare`, `citations`, `unresolved_note`, `writer_references`, `select_pages` and `normalize_source` call none. The judge has two uses in a job: in preparation, to compare the prepared description with the source, and in each numbered iteration, to compare the prepared description $M$ with that iteration's prose. The optional `conversation_reply` calls the conversation role through `call` and, for a stage file the turn names, the prose writer through `prose_write`.

## 3 The sequence inside `cycle`

`cycle` runs `prepare` first. It searches from the source material's concepts, writes the prepared model prose $M$, and checks the prose against the source. A preparation with unresolved questions returns `needs_input` unless the request set `force`, in which case the source material is used as $M$ and the questions and writer's assumptions are retained. The structure stated in $M$ must fit the requested target; otherwise the job returns a question before writing files. When $M$ names an income process only by a general distribution function, `verify_distribution` asks whether its law is specified. The initial `write` then writes $D$ from $M$ and elaborates $D$ to $E$. A supplied `starting_declaration` takes the place of this initial writer call; if the elaborator refuses it, the refusal is passed to `write` for repair. The initial writing is recorded separately, as `initial_writing`, at cycle 0.

For each numbered iteration, `prose_write` reads the dossier of the preceding elaborated semantic object and produces new prose. The judge compares that prose with the prepared $M$ for a diagnostic report. `write` then writes one new declaration from the new prose and elaborates it. `compare` checks this elaborated semantic object against the preceding one. Thus iteration 1 is $E \to M' \to D' \to E'$ and compares $E$ with $E'$. If they differ, iteration 2 is $E' \to M'' \to D'' \to E''$ and compares $E'$ with $E''$. It does not repeat `write(M)` or produce an extra $E'''$ in iteration 2. A successful comparison ends the job `converged` with the reason `semantic_fixed_point`; otherwise the next iteration begins from the latest $E$, up to the cycle limit. `citations` checks the declaration's quotations against the prose from which that declaration was written, and `unresolved_note` checks its writer's note. Those results and both prose judges' reports remain available for review; none decides convergence.

A writing whose last answer requests material after its allowed request rounds ends `not_converged` with `request_rounds_exhausted`. If an iteration cannot produce an elaborated semantic object, it records no comparison for that iteration; the latest successful writing remains available. A call refused at a language-model token ceiling ends `not_converged` with `token_ceiling_reached`, retaining the calls and writings recorded so far. A cancellation stops before the next language-model call, after any call already sent finishes.

Inside `prepare` and `write`, every call to a role goes through `_requests`. Each message holds the cached prefix (the instruction text and the guide pages the settings name), then the reading controller's parts (the retrieved passages, the addresses the role may request with its remaining allowances, and the material its requests added with their outcomes, each item once), then the task's input, the formulation, the job's target and, for the writer, the structure the description states, and any correction feedback, which stand in every message of the invocation. `_requests` examines each answer for the request form before the role's own parsers; a request is answered by the controller, which consumes a request round and no ordinary round, and the role is called again with the result; the first ordinary answer goes to the role's parsers and consumes one of `preparer_rounds` or `writer_rounds`. When the role answers with a request and no request round, or no ordinary round, remains, the controller executes nothing and `_requests` returns `None`, which ends the invocation with `request_rounds_exhausted`.

The writer returns the files of the job's target and no other, which the job frame's `elaborate_files(files, target)` checks: for a stage, one stage file `<key>.bl` and the note `note.md`, elaborated as one text; for a period, the stage files and `period.yml`, elaborated as a trellis of that period repeated twice and then held, printed and compared as its one period; for a trellis, the stage files, a period file for each period template, `period.yml` or `period.<name>.yml`, and `trellis.yml`, elaborated as a trellis; and for a recipe, the files of a trellis, the recipe `spec.yml`, a methods file `stages/<key>/methods.yml` for each stage the description says how to solve, `calibration/<model>.yml` and `settings/<model>.yml`, written into a temporary folder in the model folder's layout and read by the Bellman factory's `load_recipe` with no fill, then elaborated and bound by `specify_trellis` with the kinds of binding the recipe holds, so that a calibration whose slot `$fill` stands unbound, or a stage without a methods file, is checked for what it holds. A recipe is held, printed and compared as the trellis of its elaborated stages, since its methods, calibration and settings are read from files and not written back into prose. A converged job's `artifacts` place each stage file under `declaration/stages/<key>/` and every other file but the note under `declaration/` at the path its name writes, which puts a recipe's methods files in their stages' folders.

The `initial_writing` record holds the first declaration's rounds, files, citations and digest. Each numbered cycle holds its prose writing, judging, one declaration writing, comparison, citation check and writer's-note check. The job runner reports `preparation`, `writing`, `prose_writing` and `judging`; initial writing uses cycle 0. Every language-model call is recorded with its role, message digest and length, answer digest, token counts and any provider failure. The job runner's progress callback raises `JobCancelled` before the next call when cancellation was requested, so a call already sent finishes and no further one starts. The processor records under `reading` the identities of the guide pages and index passages each call received, the searches it made and the outcome of requests; the record holds no message text.

The job store keeps this record whole, and `GET /v1/model-iterations/{job_id}` returns one of two views. The default products view holds the schema, target, structure, status, reason, prepared prose (`description`), questions, paper checks and `cycles_run`, the count of numbered iterations. `available_writing` holds the latest nonempty parsed files, their cycle number (0 for the initial declaration), and the prose from which they were written; `last_cycle` holds the final numbered iteration's files, judging and adjacent comparison, which may be absent if that iteration stopped early. `last_completed_comparison` identifies the latest pair actually compared by left and right cycle numbers, even when the final iteration stopped before comparison. `reading` is reduced to index and guide digests, and `usage` to calls and tokens charged. The full view, `view=full`, holds `initial_writing` and all numbered cycles for examination or training. `products_view` in `processor.py` derives the default view from that record.

For a period, trellis or recipe, the elaboration bridge reads Bellman's selected connections once from the stage occurrences already constructed. The dossier names the resolved stage and field at each selected connection, including connections between periods. Its digest records the ordered stage occurrences, those connections and the effective parameter values in each period. The deterministic comparison checks the connections and effective values under one matching of the stage occurrences and their names. A connection or route that Bellman cannot certify, including a backward value type that does not match, is returned as a refusal before the two declarations can compare equal. A complete recipe uses the trellis returned after its requested bindings have been applied. If `$fill` still leaves a parameter without a value in a period, the digest and dossier distinguish known source values from bound values and name the unresolved period. Matching partial recipes can compare equal as declarations; the missing value remains unbound.

## 4 The job frame a Matsya handler receives

The job frame is one record, the dataclass `Frame` of `frame.py`, built by the processor for each job; the word frame alone names the package. A Matsya handler uses the `bellman` package and the `meaning-map-training` package only through the job frame and imports neither.

| Attribute | What it is |
|---|---|
| `texts` | the seven instruction texts by role key, and the conversation role's when the configuration enables it |
| `guides`, `guide_manifest` | the three guides by short name (`writer`, `reader`, `calculus`) and their entries of the page manifest: the ordered pages and, for a manifest of the form `matsya-guides/3`, each saved page's sections, authored links and tag memberships |
| `settings` | the checked settings, with `roles` as `RoleSettings` records and `ceilings`, the language-model token ceilings, as a `Ceilings` record |
| `call(role, parts, prefix)` | the function that sends a message to the provider: the ordered parts of one message and the number of leading parts marked for the prompt cache; returns the answer with its token counts. It is the method `call` of the fixed caller `ProviderCaller` of `roles.py`, which counts the message's tokens first, refuses a message that would not fit the language model's context window with `PromptTooLong` and a call that would pass a token ceiling with `CeilingReached`, and charges every attempt to the job's token ledger |
| `record`, `progress` | the job's record, begun by the processor, and the job runner's progress callback |
| `index`, `source`, `references`, `earlier` | the reading controller `ReadingController` of `retrieval/context.py`, which the processor builds over the snapshot of the retrieval index the job holds and which is a Matsya handler's only access to the index and to the guides' records: `bind` binds it to the role of an invocation of `prepare` or `write`, `search` makes the invocation's initial search, `reuse` places passages an earlier invocation retrieved, `request_of` and `resolve` examine an answer for the request form and answer a request through the snapshot's `related` and `read` ([The retrieval index](01-the-retrieval-index.md) §9) or the guides' records, `parts` gives the parts it writes into the next message, and `called`, `ordinary` and `end` record the calls, the ordinary answers and why the invocation stopped ([What Matsya reads](02-what-matsya-reads.md) §5); the job's `Source` record; the passages of preparation's initial search; and `earlier`, the job of the same session that this job continues, with its identity, status, source digest, description, questions, formulation and concepts, or `None` |
| `join_parts`, `sha256` | the joining of a message's parts under `===== <label> =====` lines, and the digest function |
| `elaborate_text`, `elaborate_trellis`, `period_joins`, `dossier_lines` | the four functions of the `bellman` package that the frame calls: the elaboration of one text and of a trellis, the joins of a period, and the dossier printer |
| `elaborate_files`, `dossier_of`, `parse_writer_files`, `digest_of`, `profile_equal`, `allowed_declaration_name`, `form_check` (`check_reader_form` in the module), `compare` (`compare_outcomes` in the module) | the functions of `meaning.py`: parse a Bellman-SYM writer's answer into files, elaborate them (`elaborate_files`, which computes the Bellman-SYM elaborator `BellmanSYMDeclaration_to_ElaboratedSemanticObject`), print the dossier with the stage names hidden, compare two elaborations with the comparator of `packages/meaning-map-training` (`compare`, which computes `ElaboratedSemanticObject_comparator`), check a description's form |
| `targets` | the four targets of a job, `stage`, `period`, `trellis` and `recipe`, in the order in which each holds the one before it (`TARGETS` of `meaning.py`), which the Matsya handler `cycle` reads to decide whether the job's target holds the level of the structure the description states |
| `errors` | the exception classes a Matsya handler raises or catches: `ModelAnswerError`, `MeaningRefusal`, `IncompleteResponse`, `PromptTooLong`, `ProviderError`, `ProviderRefusal`, `ProviderRejected`, and the set `STOP_REASONS` |

For a turn of conversation mode the turn runner fills the same record: `index` is the reading controller bound to the turn (§7), `source` is `None`, `record` holds the turn's identifiers, and `call` is the fixed caller's, wrapped so that the turn runner records each call and its events itself; the call list a Matsya handler receives for a turn is its own and no record reads it.

A Matsya handler raises `ModelAnswerError` when a language model's answer lacks the required structure or evidence, with one of the 27 validation codes the program fixes, such as `json_decode`, `judge_quotes` or `writer_input_quote`; the job runner records such an error as `invalid_model_response`.

## 5 How the program loads and checks the module

`configuration.load` runs its checks in a fixed order and stops at the first failure: the files against `manifest.json`; the three guides against their page manifest, the page markers and, for a manifest of the form `matsya-guides/3`, each page's saved digest, its sections' spans and the targets of its available links; `settings.json`; the import of the Matsya handler module under the module name `matsya_configuration_handlers`; and the thirteen signatures, with `conversation_reply` when the settings name the conversation role, each name present and callable with exactly the slot's parameter names in the slot's order, read with `inspect.signature`. Type annotations, default values and return types are not compared, and no other function is checked, so helpers may be added, renamed or removed freely, and a configuration that does not name the conversation role may hold a function named `conversation_reply`, which is then not checked. Before the signatures the loader reads the conversation role's parts together, after the settings' types and before the readers of `roles`, `ceilings` and `reading`: the role's `reading` entry, its entry in `ceilings.roles`, `ceilings.turn`, `conversation_rounds` and `conversation_pages`, then the text `texts/conversation-answer.md`; a part missing with the role, or present without it, refuses the folder naming that part. A failed check raises `ConfigurationRefused`, whose detail names the file or the Matsya handler: when a check fails at the start of a job, the job fails with the code `configuration_refused` and no language model is called; when it fails before the server starts, `matsya-serve` refuses to start. The folder is checked on three occasions on a server: by `matsya-serve` before the server starts, by the application's start-up, and at the start of every job, so an edit to the folder takes effect at the next job. Two commands act on the folder: `python -m matsya_service.configuration manifest <folder> --version <version>` rewrites `manifest.json` from the files present, and `python -m matsya_service.configuration check <folder>` loads the folder as a job would and prints its version and digest, then a line that names the form of the guides' page manifest and says whether its relationship records were checked, or `configuration_refused` with the detail.

## 6 Changing a Matsya handler, adding a helper, adding a slot

To change what a Matsya handler does while keeping its name and parameter names, edit the module, rewrite the manifest with the `manifest` command, and run the `check` command. The package's tests do not load the configuration folder that the service uses: they copy a second configuration kept for the tests under `packages/matsya/tests/fixtures/configuration/`, whose Matsya handler module and eight texts, the conversation role's among them, are byte-identical with the service's folder on 9 October 2026, so a change to the service's module is tested only when the same change is made to the test copy. The test copy's guides are written by the guide builder from the small documentation tree of `packages/matsya/tests/fixtures/documentation/`, which holds a stand-in for each core page and the pages that test the links and tags, under that tree's own construction policy.

Adding a helper needs only that it be written in the module and called from one of the thirteen Matsya handlers, since the program neither calls nor checks it.

Adding a slot, renaming one, changing a slot’s parameter names, adding a role or adding a setting changes the program. Each requires coordinated changes to the program and configuration. An optional slot, such as `conversation_reply`, stands in `OPTIONAL_SLOTS` with the role that requires it in `ROLE_SLOTS`, an optional role in `OPTIONAL_ROLES` of `roles.py` and its settings in `OPTIONAL_SETTINGS`, and the loader checks them only in a configuration that names the role. A new slot needs its entry with ordered parameter names in `SLOTS` of `configuration.py`, a call to it from the program or from another Matsya handler, the function in every configuration's module including the test copy, rewritten manifests, and a corresponding row in the handler table in §2. A new role needs its key in `ROLES` of `roles.py`, a text `texts/<role key with hyphens for underscores>.md`, an entry in the `roles` object of `settings.json`, and a Matsya handler that calls `call(frame, "<role key>", parts, calls, None)`. A new setting needs its key and type in `SETTINGS` of `configuration.py`, the key in `settings.json`, and a Matsya handler that reads `frame.settings["<key>"]`, because the loader refuses a key present in only one of the two.

A trained candidate is a new configuration folder with its own manifest and version, and a candidate whose Matsya handler module differs from its parent's is recorded as a commit together with the run that accepted it.

## 7 One turn of conversation mode

A configuration that enables the conversation role, `conversation_answer`, answers a question asked in a session in a **turn**, which the route `POST /v1/sessions/{session_id}/turns` appends to the session as an entry of the user and records as `queued`. The turn runner, `TurnRunner` of `conversation.py`, which the program holds and a training pass does not change, then loads the configuration, fixes the turn's snapshot of the index, builds the turn's language-model token ledger, binds the reading controller ([What Matsya reads](02-what-matsya-reads.md) §8), reads again the passages the previous turn cited, elaborates a stage file the request names, and calls `conversation_reply(frame, turn, calls)`. `turn` is the record `Turn` of `conversation.py`: the turn's identifier and session, the question and its entry number, the named fields, the elaborated stage file or its refusal, never the file's text, the session's entries up to the question, the previous turn's decision list, the retained passages and the snapshot's digest.

The starting `conversation_reply` places the guide pages `conversation_pages` names, which the controller reports as `frame.index.pages`, after the instruction text as the cached prefix; makes one search from the question through `frame.index.search`; has the controller supply, with `frame.index.supply_job`, the outputs of the job the conversation discusses, which the turn runner names: the session's selected job or else its latest job that holds a record and was current at its end, never a job that was not current unless the selection names it; the label of each output of a job that was not current states the version of the session's text the job was built from and the version the text is at now, the turn's result names the job under `job`, and the job's stage files are never supplied this way; and, for a stage file that elaborated, calls `prose_write` on the elaborated semantic object, so that the role receives the dossier and the prose writer's description of it. It then calls the role through `_requests`, as `prepare` and `write` call theirs, so that every answer is examined for the request form before any other parser and a request is resolved by the controller within the role's request rounds. An ordinary answer is read as one JSON object with `_json_answer` and checked: its status and fields, each citation with `frame.index.check_citation`, the check the turn runner makes again, and the form of each item of the decision list. A failed answer is sent back with `your previous answer` and `the faults of your previous answer` until `conversation_rounds` answers have been given. The Matsya handler returns `answered` with the answer, and with `submit_job` when the role starts a job at the user's request, checked against `cycle_maximum`, or `needs_input` with the role's questions, each with its citations and decision list, or `incomplete` with `request_rounds_exhausted` or `conversation_rounds_exhausted`, no citation and the previous decision list, and records why the invocation stopped with `frame.index.end`.

The turn runner checks the return: a return of another form ends the turn `failed` with `processing_failed`; a citation of material not supplied in the turn, or with a quote absent from it, ends the turn `incomplete` with `unsupported_citation`; an `incomplete` status must agree with the turn's record of allowances, ceilings and calls; and a call refused at a ceiling, or one the provider could not complete, ends the turn `incomplete` whatever `conversation_reply` returns or raises. It decides whether the turn is current, appends the answer, or the questions, as entries whose origin is the turn and which reply to the question, and stores the record. The turn's states, statuses, reasons, fixed sentences and record are the program's, so that a Matsya handler can change what the role reads and how its answers are repaired, but not the permissions, the snapshot, the limits or the acceptance of an answer.
