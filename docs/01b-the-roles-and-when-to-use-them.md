# The roles of Matsya and when to use them

> [!summary]
> Matsya's work is done by language-model **roles**, each with a fixed instruction text, a fixed input and a fixed output, called by the program in a fixed order; nothing else in Matsya decides anything about economics. This page names the roles, says what each receives and returns, and then says which command a user, or the assistant working for the user, runs for each kind of request: a question, a build, a reply to the architect's question, a reading of the result, a stop. The three parts of Matsya are defined in [Matsya in three parts](index.md); the commands in [Working with Matsya from the command line](01-working-from-the-command-line.md).

## 1 The roles of a job

A job of matsya-architect runs its roles in a fixed order: the Model-prose-writer and the prose-source-judge once, in preparation, and the others in cycles. Each role is a language model with one instruction text; the two judges share one instruction text and differ in the pair of texts each is given, and the report's table of language-model tokens shows them in one row. What a role may read is fixed by the program, and what it returns is checked by the program before anything else happens.

| Role | Receives | Returns | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Model-prose-writer** | the source material: the session's text or the submitted file, with the passages of the index that match it and the pages of the reference guides | the **model prose**: the model restated as a paper's model section under fixed headings (states, controls, shocks and their timing, laws of motion, the recursion, the condition that determines the policy, the parameters), or questions when the source leaves a necessary thing unsaid | to fix what model is meant before anything is written in the language |
| **prose-source-judge** | the model prose and the source material | for each of ten economic items, from the state space and the timing of information to the terminal condition and the parameters, whether the prose omits, adds or contradicts something the source says, with the sentences quoted; it reads the two texts in both orders | to stop, before anything is written in the language, a restatement that departs from what the author wrote |
| **Prose-to-Bellman-Sym** | the model prose, the pages of the language guide, the passages found in preparation and those it requests, and, in a later cycle, what kept the previous cycle from converging: the prose-roundtrip-judge's disagreements, the round-trip comparison, the quotations not found in the model prose and the open note | the **stage files** of the declaration, in Bellman-SYM, each block quoting in a `# from:` line the sentence of the model prose it rests on, with a note of what the prose left open and of any assumption it supplied, which reads `none` when nothing is open; a period file and a trellis file when the model has several stages | to write the declaration; it builds it from the prose and copies no example. In each cycle it writes the files a second time, from the round-trip prose alone, for the round-trip comparison |
| the **elaborator** (a program, not a role) | the stage files | acceptance, or a refusal naming the rule, the line and what to write instead, which goes back to Prose-to-Bellman-Sym for repair | to check that the files parse and satisfy the rules of the language |
| the **distribution check** | the model prose with an income or shock variable whose probability distribution the writer could not write, or an income the prose describes only by a general distribution function, and the reason | whether the prose specifies that probability distribution, with the sentences quoted, and otherwise one question for the user | to ask the user, before or during writing, for a probability distribution the prose leaves unspecified, instead of letting the writer supply one |
| **Semantics-to-Prose** | the elaborated declaration, as the program prints it, and nothing of the source or of the model prose | the **round-trip prose**: the model as the declaration states it, under the same headings | to read back what was written, without seeing what was meant |
| **prose-roundtrip-judge** | the model prose and the round-trip prose | for each of the same ten items, whether the two agree, with the sentences quoted | to decide whether the declaration says what the prose meant |
| the **round-trip comparison** (a program, not a role) | the stage files written from the model prose and those written again from the round-trip prose, both elaborated | whether the two declare the same stages, up to the names chosen, with the first difference when they do not | to check that the declaration and the files written from its reading back state the same model |

Preparation runs once: the Model-prose-writer writes the model prose, in up to three attempts, and the prose-source-judge compares it with the source, its disagreements going back to the Model-prose-writer for the next attempt; if the model prose still departs from the source after the last attempt, the job ends `not_converged`, with the reason `paper_description_differs_from_source`, before any stage file is written. A cycle ends the job `converged` when the stage files written again from the round-trip prose equal the written ones, the prose-roundtrip-judge agrees on every item, each block of the stage files quotes at least one sentence and every sentence it quotes occurs in the model prose, and the writer's note reads `none`; otherwise what failed goes to Prose-to-Bellman-Sym and the next cycle begins, up to the number of cycles allowed. A job that reaches that number ends `not_converged`, with the reason `cycle_limit_reached`, and its report says what still did not match, with each item on which a judge disagrees under a heading of its own. A job also ends `not_converged` before its cycles are spent when the elaborator still refuses the stage files after the writer's attempts (`stage_file_refused`), when a role has used up its requests for more pages or passages (`request_rounds_exhausted`), or when a call would pass a ceiling of language-model tokens (`token_ceiling_reached`); the report's section Status names the reason. A job that stopped at a question, the Model-prose-writer's or the distribution check's, ends `needs_input`; the questions stand in the session for the user to answer when the job has a session and the user added no entry to it while the job ran, and otherwise in the job's record alone, where `matsya job status <job>` prints them.

## 2 The role of a turn

matsya-master has one role of its own, the **conversation role**; when a question names a stage file that the elaborator accepts, it also calls Semantics-to-Prose, to describe that file. The conversation role receives the question, pages of the reference guides, the passages of the index that match the question, the session's text, the outputs of the job it discusses, which [Working with Matsya from the command line](01-working-from-the-command-line.md) §6 defines, and, when the question names a stage file, that file elaborated and printed by the program with Semantics-to-Prose's account of it, or the elaborator's refusal; it may ask the program for more pages, more passages, the outputs of the session's other jobs or a job's stage files a bounded number of times. It returns an answer with citations, each a quoted sentence of what it read, and the program accepts no citation it cannot find in that material. It writes no stage file. When the user asks it to build or to submit, it sets one field of its reply and the program starts a job from the session; it starts no job otherwise.

## 3 Which command for which request

| The request | What runs | The command |
|---|---|---|
| a question about a model, about the language, about why a judge disagreed, about what a job produced | one turn of the conversation role, a few calls of a language model, taking less time and costing less than a job | `matsya ask <session> "…"` |
| build a declaration from a description | one job of all the roles of §1, several minutes, charged by the language-model text of every call | `matsya job submit model.md` (a session is created for the file) |
| rebuild it after an edit of the description | a new job, as above, in the same session: the edited text is appended to the session, and the job reads the session's whole text, the earlier description included | `matsya job submit model.md --session <session>`; a second `matsya job submit model.md` would create a second session |
| build from the discussion so far | the same job, started by the conversation role from the session's text | `matsya ask <session> "Submit this as a job."` |
| answer a question the architect asked | the reply appended to the session; the first reply to the job's questions starts the next job with the reply in its text, unless a later job of the session has started | `matsya session add <session> reply.md --replies-to <entry>` |
| read what a job produced, or its report of what did not match | nothing on the server; the client writes the folder and the report | `matsya job files <job> <folder>` |
| see the jobs of a model, their labels and the selected job, when one is selected; with none selected, the listing does not name the job the conversation discusses, which [Working with Matsya from the command line](01-working-from-the-command-line.md) §6 defines | nothing on the server; a listing | `matsya session show <session>` |
| make the conversation discuss another job of the session, one that holds a record | a mark on the session | `matsya session select <session> <job>` |
| stop a job that is queued or running | a queued job is cancelled at once; a running job ends before its next call, and a call already sent is charged | `matsya job cancel <job>` |
| search the documentation, and the literature your Matsya token may read, without a question | the index alone, no role | `matsya search "…"`, and `matsya search "…" --collections literature` for the literature |

Two rules follow for an assistant acting for a user. A job costs money and minutes and is started only when the user asks to build; a question costs little and is the way to understand a result before building again. And a job's questions are answered in the session, all of them in one reply file, not by editing the files: the next job reads the session's text, and the first reply starts it at once. A later reply to the same questions starts nothing, and one that arrives while that job runs is not in its text, so the job ends marked `built from an earlier version` and appends no question to the session.
